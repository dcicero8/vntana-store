Step 1. Materials: use the Material Library, by name

The materials are finished Blender materials in the Taco Material Library workspace. Each item shows a preview ball. The real material is in the item's Attachments: a Blender file <NAME>_v<N>.blend with textures packed, and a text file <NAME>_v<N>.json with the instructions. Always take the highest version number.

  1. Download the .blend and the .json of the material.
  2. Read the .json first. Follow how_to_apply and requirements exactly.
  3. In your file: File, Append, open the material .blend, Material, pick the material by its name.
  4. Assign it to the part in the table. Do not change any value in it.

Step 2. Close the casing joint (no gasket)

Taco's CAD leaves a groove between the pump casing and the black motor bracket, where a gasket sits on the real pump. Taco wants it closed and flush, with no gasket modeled. On the 0018e-2F4 the numbers were as below. Blender coordinates, millimeters. Measure each model; the other bodies may differ slightly.

  1. Fill the step on the bracket. The black motor bracket (the object named shaft) has a 0.5 mm step all around its outer edge. Raise those edge vertices to the level of the bracket's main face (from Y 2.35 to Y 2.85; 248 vertices).
  2. Seat the casing. Move Pump_Body toward the motor until its mating face sits on the bracket face (0.22 mm along minus Y).
  3. Move the four casing bolts with it (the part of Fastener_black that holds the casing), so the bolt heads stay seated.
Check: look straight from the side and straight from the top against a white background. No light may pass between the casing and the bracket.

Step 4. Stainless bodies: cast silver, with a machined flange face

  • Cast faces: TACO_STAINLESS_CAST version 2 on the whole stainless body.
  • Machined sealing face: on both flanges, the flat sealing face, the gasket groove (floor and walls) and the bore chamfer get TACO_STAINLESS_MACHINED. Give those faces their own material slot. Cut the boundary on mesh edges so it is a clean circle. The flange outer wall and the ears stay cast.
  • Four attributes on that mesh make the tool lines run in circles around the pipe: turn_axis, turn_origin, turned = 1 and cut = 0. The exact types and a ready script are in the material's .json.
  • Only stainless is machined. The top of a cast iron flange is plain cast. Taco confirmed this on Nov 13.
  • Status: the machined face goes to Taco for review on Nov 20. You can do everything else on the stainless models now, and add the machined face when VNTANA confirms.
  • In the web model the machined face is a plain bright ring. The tool lines show in renders only.

Step 5. The web model (GLB)

The platform flattens fine grain that is baked into one large map for a whole part. So the grain goes into the GLB as a small tile that repeats. The gold standard GLB is the working example; open it and copy the setup.

  • Plain values for color, metal and roughness on the library materials. No baked color maps for them.
  • Cast iron grain and rubber stipple: add a second UV set on the part, projected from six sides in real size, and use the library's normal tile on it, set to repeat. Cast iron: one tile per 32 mm. Rubber cap: one tile per 40 mm. Keep the first UV set for anything else on the part.
  • Black plastic and rubber have reduced surface reflection. Export it as the glTF specular setting (Blender does this when the material is exported as is).
  • Do not bake the grain into the part's own normal map.

Step 6. Delivery

  • New version of the existing asset. Never a new asset, and never overwrite the current file. The older versions stay.
  • One Blender file per asset. Delete the old attached .blend, then attach the new one, with a new version in the file name. Save in Blender 5.1.
  • Processing: choose the optimization preset Taco Default (150,000 polygons, inside parts kept, textures up to 2048). Keeping the inside parts is what makes the exploded view work.
  • Viewer: set the viewer preset to Taco Default on the new version.
  • Order of work: the cast iron e-series first (0015e3-2F2, 0015e3-2F4, 0018e-2F2, 007e-2F2, 007e3-2F4), then the stainless e-series. For the 0026e, 0034e, 0034e+ and VT2218, apply the same materials and check the casing joint; close it the same way if there is a groove.
Checklist for each circulator: real size at scale 1 · library materials on body, terminal box, round cap and arrow · box and cap are two materials · old baked cast iron normal map removed · Nov 6 faceplate art · joint closed, no light passing · side sticker removed · GLB uses repeating tiles · one Blender file attached · uploaded as a new version with Taco Default processing and viewer preset.

What to model

  • One bronze material does both finishes. TACO_BRONZE_CAST version 2 covers the rough cast body and the smoother machined faces. On each bronze mesh add a face-corner attribute named machined: 1 on machined faces (pipe openings, thread ends, the vent stub, the seat under the magnet collar), 0 on cast faces. Without the attribute the whole part renders as rough cast.
  • Model the threads on the threaded versions, and make the seam between the cap and the body smooth. The test model had a jagged seam; that was the test mesh, not the target.
  • Something must be visible inside. The separators are filled with pall rings: small stamped stainless rings with curled tabs, held in a wire cage in the larger tanks. Model one pall ring from the photos and place enough of them that the inside does not look empty through the openings. You do not need to model every ring.
  • Magnetic dirt separator: it has a removable black magnet collar around the neck, sitting on a smooth machined band, and a green label band on the body. See the photos.
  • Bright polished parts (the press fitting and the band under the magnet collar) look shinier than the library's machined setting. Mark them as machined for now and flag them in your QA notes. VNTANA will confirm whether they need their own material.
  • Labels: Taco is sending the green label art. Leave the band ready for a texture, with one consistent start and end point on every size.

3Rules of Delivery

Every asset, every family. If a rule cannot be met on a given part, flag it in your QA notes rather than guessing. This section is the acceptance standard: assets that miss these are returned.

New Latest from Taco (Oct 2): the image angle reference guide is confirmed, see Render / template setup below. The 007e-family faceplate is being redesigned across multiple departments (target end of October), so faceplate artwork is on hold. Engineering is checking whether SolidWorks source files are available (update expected Thursday). Contacts: Ken owns materials / art direction; Jess & Jai own angles / render outputs.

What you deliver (per asset)

  • GLB (glTF binary, PBR metal/roughness, textures embedded). This is the primary deliverable. New Export it from the delivered .blend: same meshes, same names, same materials. A GLB merged into one mesh or one material is returned.
  • Packed Blender file (.blend) with all textures packed in.
  • Parts manifest (CSV): one row per serviceable part, with the real part name.
  • Substance source files: Updated Nov 13 no longer needed. Do not deliver .spp, .sbs or .sbsar files.
  • Self-check renders New: the hero shot plus one 4K close-up of the cast metal, as PNGs.
  • 4K texture maps folder: no longer needed; the maps are packed in the .blend.

Geometry

  • One mesh per serviceable part, placed at the scene root. No empty wrappers, no nested parent hierarchy around parts.
  • No mega-part. Do not merge the whole product into a single mesh.
  • Keep all internal parts. Do not delete internals just because they are hidden in the assembled view.
  • High-poly is the primary deliverable and must stay smooth at 4K. Do not pre-decimate.
  • Nested shells (a part inside a housing) need at least 2mm clearance so they do not poke through after optimization.
  • Flag artifacts, do not reproduce them. If the source CAD has a defect, note it; do not model the defect faithfully.

Naming

  • Real part names on every mesh. Use the replacement-parts naming where available.
  • For the first run, group the obvious hardware (screws, bolts, washers) into a single named group per fastener type. We will revisit finer renaming later. Build it logically now.
Note on part names: STEP AP203 export strips component names, so circulators arrive as unnamed solids. Where you cannot get a real name from CAD, name from the catalog / replacement-parts sheet and flag it.

Origin, scale, orientation

  • Upright and grounded. Origin at bottom-center of the product.
  • Real-world scale in meters.
  • No animations, curves, constraints, or empties in the delivered file.
  • New No scale on any object. Apply all transforms (scale 1.0 everywhere). Never scale the product to fit a camera; move the camera instead.

Materials & UVs

  • glTF PBR metal/roughness, textures embedded in the GLB.
  • One material per physical material. Do not split one physical material across several, or merge two real materials into one. New In the .blend this means separate Blender materials taken from the signed-off library, named as in the library, not regions of one baked atlas. Cast metals must show real sand-cast grain at true scale.
  • Single 0 to 1 UV space per material, with seams hidden on non-visible faces.
  • Faceplates and labels are textures, not modeled geometry. Confirm the version/label artwork before baking it in.
  • Side-of-casing label. Decided Oct 9 For 0034e and 0026e the side marking is a sticker label: deliver the Flat Label version only, using the side label layout (.ai), never the old CAD text. Put the label text in its own material on the label part (Taco may ask for lighter text). Keep the casing shape as the CAD has it. Other families: ask before building the label; some newer products are etched.
Updated Nov 13 Faceplate art is final. Use Taco's files dated Nov 6 from the 3D Toolkit, as listed in section A, step 3. Side sticker: Taco no longer wants it on the circulators; see section A, step 3. Second UV set: the "single UV space" rule above still holds for the first UV set; the repeating grain tiles use a second set, see section A, step 5.
