3Rules of Delivery

Every asset, every family. If a rule cannot be met on a given part, flag it in your QA notes rather than guessing. This section is the acceptance standard: assets that miss these are returned.

What you deliver (per asset)

  • GLB (glTF binary, PBR metal/roughness, textures embedded). This is the primary deliverable.
  • Packed Blender file (.blend) with all textures packed in.
  • Parts manifest (CSV): one row per serviceable part, with the real part name.
  • 4K texture maps (source resolution, before embedding).

Geometry

  • One mesh per serviceable part, placed at the scene root. No empty wrappers, no nested parent hierarchy around parts.
  • No mega-part. Do not merge the whole product into a single mesh.
  • Keep all internal parts. Do not delete internals just because they are hidden in the assembled view.
  • High-poly is the primary deliverable and must stay smooth at 4K. Do not pre-decimate.
  • Nested shells (a part inside a housing) need at least 2mm clearance so they do not poke through after optimization.
  • Flag artifacts, do not reproduce them. If the source CAD has a defect, note it; do not model the defect faithfully.

Naming

  • Real part names on every mesh. Use the replacement-parts naming where available.
  • For the first run, group the obvious hardware (screws, bolts, washers) into a single named group per fastener type. We will revisit finer renaming later. Build it logically now.
Note on part names: STEP AP203 export strips component names, so circulators arrive as unnamed solids. Where you cannot get a real name from CAD, name from the catalog / replacement-parts sheet and flag it.

Origin, scale, orientation

  • Upright and grounded. Origin at bottom-center of the product.
  • Real-world scale in meters.
  • No animations, curves, constraints, or empties in the delivered file.

Materials & UVs

  • glTF PBR metal/roughness, textures embedded in the GLB.
  • One material per physical material. Do not split one physical material across several, or merge two real materials into one.
  • Single 0 to 1 UV space per material, with seams hidden on non-visible faces.
  • Faceplates and labels are textures, not modeled geometry. Confirm the version/label artwork before baking it in.
  • Side-of-casing label, build it both ways. New Taco is not yet sure whether the side marking is etched into the casing or a flat sticker/label applied on top, so deliver both: one version with the artwork etched into the surface, and one with it as a flat label sitting on the casing. Some fields (MN / PN / JO / DC) are machine-generated at assembly and appear as XXXXXX placeholders. Use the side label layout (.ai working file) for placement and keep the X-string fields as shown.
