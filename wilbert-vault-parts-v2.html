Components

1
Vault Cover (Lid)
Concrete (3D)
Concrete cover that seals to the base. Molded as one solid piece: model it as a single mesh even though the DWG shows multiple pieces. The lid is the hard part (angles, notches, holes, debossed logo); the box itself is quick.
2
Carapace (cover top / print surface)
Plastic (3D) · print is 2D
Plastic piece on top of the cover. This is where the customer print or graphic goes. Default is the plain white / marble look. Model the plate; the print itself is a swappable 2D map, not heavy geometry.
3
Cover Print
Decal (2D)
Legacy Series™ / Legacy Custom™ print applied on the carapace. Flat mesh plane, swappable image, not geometry.
4
Wilbert Venetian Logo (deboss)
Concrete relief (3D) · model it
The Wilbert Venetian name is debossed (recessed) into the concrete cover. Not in the Creo file, so model the recess; drop the logo art in as 2D. Standoff nameplates (true 3D plates) appear on other models, not the White Venetian.
5
Vault Base (Body)
Concrete (3D)
Lower concrete receptacle that houses the casket. One mesh, bottom-center origin. Concrete base + concrete cover are the only concrete parts.
6
Liners (carapace + cover + base) (interior, seen with cover off)
Plastic (3D)
Three plastic parts: carapace, cover liner, base liner. The liner is white inside, which is what makes it a "White" Venetian. Use the current liner only; one liner fits all for our purposes (the customer does not choose the liner).
7
Emblem (not on the White Venetian V1)
Hardware (3D)
Metal emblems glued to the carapace on higher models (13 designs plus Veteran, in bronze / copper / stainless). True 3D, modeled from imagery: Wilbert has no CAD or drawings for these. Out of scope for the White Venetian first build.
8
Side / End Pattern
Decal (2D)
The picture-frame pattern on the sides and ends. In reality it is a debossed relief in the concrete, but Wilbert approved keeping it a 2D map so the mesh stays light (modeling it crashes the CAD). Foot label is likewise a flat 2D plate.

Vault line by tier (build phasing)

Wilbert groups the vaults into three tiers. Only the White Venetian is in the current build (Phase 1) — everything else on this page is a later phase, shown for context.

Basic Protection

Concrete body + plastic liner
  • Phase 1 White Venetian — building now. White plastic liner.
  • Venetian — same body, cream liner / different color scheme.
  • Continental — flat top and round top; own handles.
  • Monticello — two-bump cover, thin molded-in handles.
  • Salute — a version of the Monticello.
Concrete base + cover, plastic liners. The cover profile (bump count) and pattern define each one. Only the White Venetian is in scope now; the rest are later.

Standard Protection

Later phase · stainless carapace
  • SST Triune — stainless carapace, fully configurable.
  • Cameo Rose — pre-configured Triune (set pink colorway).
  • Veteran — pre-configured Triune (flag nameplate + military emblem).
One Triune body; Cameo Rose and Veteran are the same geometry, just locked configurations.

Premium Protection

Later phase · top of line
  • Bronze Triune — Triune body, bronze material.
  • Copper Triune — Triune body, copper material.
  • The Wilbert Bronze — its own body (chamfered corners, plastic liner inside + outside plus a metal liner).
Bronze / Copper Triunes share the Triune geometry (material swap). The Wilbert Bronze is a distinct model.

Confirmed with Wilbert — Sept 16 & 18 calls

Build spec for the White Venetian, from the PoC sync and the CAD / Creo call with the Wilbert team.

Materials · construction

  • Only two concrete parts: the base and the cover. Everything else on the Venetian is plastic.
  • Three plastic parts: carapace (top), cover liner, base liner.
  • The cover defines the model. The Venetian cover has three bumps. White = the plastic liner inside is white.
  • Lid is one solid mesh even though the DWG and Creo break it into pieces. The lid is the only tricky part.
  • Deliver two states: a hero with the cover off and leaning, and a closed version.

Hardware

  • White Venetian has no handles. It has pickup holes at the ends instead (that is how it is lifted).
  • Other models have handles (three types across the line: molded-in bars on Monticello/Salute, distinct handles on Continental and Wilbert Bronze). Later scope.
  • Nameplate: true 3D standoff plate on some models; the White Venetian instead has the debossed Wilbert Venetian logo.

Emblems · personalization

  • Emblems are true 3D, modeled from imagery. Wilbert has no CAD or drawings for them. 13 designs plus Veteran, in bronze / copper / stainless. Not on the White Venetian V1.
  • Everything else on the top is 2D. Cover prints and appliqués are swappable flat maps on the carapace, not geometry.

Interior · scope

  • Plastic liner, white for the White Venetian. One current liner; one liner fits all for the model.
  • Side / end pattern stays 2D (a map, not modeled relief).
  • Infant vaults are out of scope. Measurements come from the STEP and DWG files.

Model these — not in the Creo CAD

  • Pickup holes at the ends (the White Venetian has holes, not handles).
  • Wilbert Venetian debossed logo recessed into the cover (logo art applied as 2D).
  • Half-moon notches at the end corners (cable-lift points; welded half-moons on the form). Keep as real geometry.
  • Side / end picture-frame pattern as a 2D map, not modeled relief (Wilbert approved 2D).

CAD & file handoff (Creo)

  • STEP files incoming: one contained full assembly, an exploded view, and each part exported individually with correct names (carapace, cover liner, base liner, cover, base). IGES is the fallback.
  • No GLB out of their Creo (checked live: only STEP / OBJ). We convert to GLB on our side.
  • Confirm units on import and expect no materials through STEP: it carries geometry only, which is all we need.
  • Delivered via SharePoint (NDA in place). Current liner only.

Still owed by Wilbert

  • Emblem art / references (no CAD exists; model from imagery when we get there).
  • Wilbert Venetian logo art for the debossed cover.
  • Higher-resolution reference images (only one product photo exists today).
  • The STEP exports from Wilbert engineering (in progress).
